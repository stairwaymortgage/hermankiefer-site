Chapter 9 of 20

Consul and Regent

1883 – 1902

Episode 9 of 20 from the documentary Kiefer: Do Right and Fear No One. Watch the full film from this chapter →

In July 1883 the exile went back to Germany — as the official representative of the United States. Dr. Kiefer served as U.S. Consul at Stettin until January 1885. His reports home included studies of the German beet-sugar industry, which the Saga credits with helping to start beet-sugar production in Michigan.

In 1889 he was elected a Regent of the University of Michigan and served until 1901. He chaired the committees on medicine and science and pushed for a four-year medical degree with an internship, a new Medical Building, an Engineering Building and a University Hospital. In 1902 the university gave him an honorary medical degree and made him Professor Emeritus.

He was also a founder of the Detroit College of Medicine, a founder and vice president of the Wayne County Savings Bank, and president of the Michigan Cremation Society.

His son Edwin painted a life-size portrait of him for the faculty room of the medical school.
